# 🥬🌕 Moon Food Guide: Canadian Space Agency Food-Production Toolkit
*What the CSA says about feeding people on the Moon, plus the real projects behind it*

## 1. The big picture

| Question | CSA's answer |
|---|---|
| Why grow food on the Moon? | The ISS gets fresh food shipments every few months. On the Moon or Mars, resupply trips will be less frequent or impossible, so crews must be more self-sufficient |
| What must space food be? | Compact, lightweight, nutritious and tasty |
| How much do astronauts eat? | Menus of about **1,900 to 3,200 calories a day**, depending on the person |
| Canada's role | A partner in NASA-led **Gateway** (lunar orbit station), and exploring ways to join advanced food production systems on the **lunar surface** |
| Benefit on Earth | The same skills help remote communities, such as Canada's North, get fresh food |

## 2. 🏆 Deep Space Food Challenge (the most Moon-ready tech)

A competition run by the CSA with NASA and Canada's Impact Canada initiative to create new food technology that **uses minimal materials, energy and water** while giving safe, nutritious, tasty food. It ran over three years, and the final jury picked its grand prize winner in **February 2024**.

### 🥇 Grand prize winner: CANGrow (Ecoation Innovative Solutions, Vancouver)
| Detail | Fact |
|---|---|
| Prize | $380,000 in grant funding |
| What it grows | Strawberries, cherry tomatoes, mycelial (fungus-based) meat substitute and more |
| Output | Potential for **over 700 kg of nutrient-dense food per year** |
| Why it fits | Minimal resources and high-quality protein, aimed at long space missions and isolated communities |

## 3. 🏠 Naurvik: a "Moon base" on Earth (Gjoa Haven, Nunavut)

*Naurvik means "the growing place" in Inuktitut.*

| Detail | Fact |
|---|---|
| Where | Gjoa Haven, about 250 km north of the Arctic Circle. Winters dip to **-40 °C** |
| What | A community-led **hydroponic** food system, with no soil needed |
| Parts | Three recycled shipping containers: **Grow Pod, Utility Pod, Power Pod** |
| Power | Almost entirely **wind and solar** |
| First harvest | Butter lettuce and microgreens |
| Started | October 2019, with upgrades planned through 2025 |
| People | Local technicians trained on the job (the page names Betty Kogvik, Mark Ullikataq, Dustin Atichok and Sammy Kogvik in a photo caption), with training that draws on **both traditional and Western knowledge** |
| Partners | Gjoa Haven community, Arctic Research Foundation, Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, National Research Council Canada, CSA |
| Related contract | The Growcer Inc. and Inuit-owned Arctic Fresh were awarded **$745,000** to co-develop a deployable growing unit |

**Why it matters for the Moon (my interpretation):** it is a real small base that must make food in a harsh place with its own power. The three pods match three modules in the Moon base concept: Grow Pod = Greenhouse, Utility Pod = Air and Water Plant, Power Pod = Power Farm.

## 7. Cautions
- This toolkit is education material, not a Moon-farming manual. It says crews "will likely have to produce food in space". No lunar-surface farm has been built yet.
- No numbers here are Moon-measured. The 700 kg per year figure is the CANGrow system's potential output as stated by the CSA.
- For how real Moon soil affects plants, see the earlier **life-science guide**: plants grew in Apollo soil but under stress.
- I did not open every linked article, video or activity. Check the original pages before teaching from them.
